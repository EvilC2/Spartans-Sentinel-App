# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Students at Sentinel Secondary (West Vancouver), grades 8 to 12, on their phones between classes, at lunch and at home. A smaller group are members of the School App Club, who run the app from the App Club section: posting trivia, polls, news and menu changes, making and giving out badges, reading feedback, and (for the Executive) running the club itself. One teacher sponsor mostly looks in and rarely acts.

## Product Purpose
Spartans is Sentinel's unofficial student app, built and run by students in the School App Club. It puts the day (rotation, bell, classes), the school's news, programs, friends and badges in one place. The App Club section has two jobs: make non-members want to join, and make running the app easy for members whatever their role. Success is students opening it daily and the club growing.

## Brand Personality
Proud, sharp, fun. Navy and gold Spartans pride without looking like an official district product. The student-facing app is playful (caf-menu look: 3D art, soft gradients); the club's back office is quieter and sharper, but every member should feel their role is cool, with more senior roles looking slightly better. The join screen is a pitch: it should make joining feel like getting in on something.

## Anti-references
- "AI slop": the same gradient hero card with blobs and a floating emoji on every page, grids of identical tinted tiles, pills on everything, hint banners stacked above content, tiny uppercase eyebrows above every section.
- Graph-paper / periodic-table novelty layouts (tried and rejected on Social).
- Anything that looks like an official West Vancouver Schools product.

## Design Principles
- Function over looks, then make the function look good. Clive said this first.
- One clear thing per screen; the role decides what that is.
- Earn decoration: celebration and art belong to moments (joining, your role card, earning a badge), not to every container.
- Same parts everywhere: one button shape, one list row, one page header.
- Plain, friendly copy. No em dashes in UI text. John Doe as the sample person.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Phone-first, iOS and Android plus a web preview. Light and dark mode. Text contrast at WCAG AA, tap targets at least 44pt, screen-reader labels on every control, nothing that only works by colour.
